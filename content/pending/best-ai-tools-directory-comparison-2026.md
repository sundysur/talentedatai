The problem is that "help you find them" and "help you choose" are different jobs, and most directories are built for the first one. They optimise for coverage, which is useful when you do not know what exists, and far less useful when you are standing in front of forty options for the same task trying to pick one.

This guide compares the main places to look, including the one we run. We will be straight about where TalentedAtAI loses, because it loses on the thing the big directories are built to win: scale. ## The Honest Framing: Coverage vs. Judgement

Every AI directory sits somewhere on a line between two extremes.

At one end is total coverage: list every tool that exists, add new ones the moment they launch, let filters and search do the sorting. This is how you get to tens of thousands of listings. The strength is obvious, you will find something for almost any task. The weakness is that a tool added in seconds is rarely tested, popularity gets confused with quality, and the default ranking is often shaped by who paid for placement.

At the other end is judgement: test a smaller number of tools, write up what each is genuinely good and bad at, and publish an opinion. The strength is that you get a real recommendation instead of a list to sift. The weakness is equally obvious, you cannot test fifty thousand tools by hand, so coverage is a fraction of what the aggregators offer.

No single site does both, because they pull in opposite directions. The right question is not "which directory is best" but "am I discovering or deciding right now." If you are discovering, breadth wins. If you are deciding, judgement wins. With that framing, here is how the main options actually compare.

## There's An AI For That: The Biggest, Built Around Tasks

There's An AI For That (TAAFT) is the largest AI tools index on the web, mapping more than 50,000 tools to over 11,000 tasks and 5,000 job roles. Its defining feature is task-based search: instead of browsing categories, you type what you want to do, like "summarise a research paper" or "generate a product video," and it returns the tools that do it.

**What it is best at:** Discovery by task. When you do not know what kind of tool you need and only know the job, TAAFT is the fastest way to a shortlist. It also runs the most-read AI newsletter, with millions of subscribers, and a timeline feature that tracks when tools launched.

**Who it is for:** Anyone starting from a task rather than a tool name, and people who want to keep a finger on what is newly launching.

**The honest limitation:** Scale is the whole proposition, and scale brings noise. With 50,000 listings, many are thinly described and untested, and the site sells sponsored placements, pay-per-click, and paid launches (named sponsors have included Slack, Wix, and GitLab). That does not make it dishonest, but it means the tools you see first are influenced by who paid, so you are discovering candidates, not getting a tested recommendation.

## Futurepedia: Discovery Plus Education

Futurepedia lists roughly 5,000 curated tools across 54 categories, and its distinguishing move is pairing the directory with an education layer: video reviews, tutorials, and structured courses, backed by a large YouTube following and newsletter.

**What it is best at:** Learning while you discover. If you want to understand a category before you commit, Futurepedia's video reviews and courses explain how tools work rather than just listing them. The category structure is cleaner and more navigable than the giant task indexes.

**Who it is for:** People newer to AI who want to build understanding alongside finding tools, and anyone who prefers video walkthroughs to reading.

**The honest limitation:** Futurepedia has moved toward a pay-to-be-featured model, where free listings get limited visibility and companies pay for prominent placement. So, as with TAAFT, the ordering you see is shaped partly by money. Its 5,000 tools are a smaller catalogue than the task-based giants, which is a plus for navigability and a minus for raw coverage.

## Toolify: The Deepest Taxonomy

Toolify indexes a very large catalogue, in the range of tens of thousands of tools, across hundreds of categories, with an emphasis on granular filtering and data like traffic rankings and revenue estimates for listed tools.

**What it is best at:** Fine-grained filtering and comparison across a huge set. If you want to slice a category by sub-type, pricing, and popularity signals, Toolify's taxonomy and data views go deeper than most.

**Who it is for:** Power users and researchers who want to analyse the market, not just find one tool, and who are comfortable with a denser, more data-heavy interface.

**The honest limitation:** More coverage and more data mean more to wade through, and the same caveat about untested listings applies. It is built for breadth and analysis, not for a plain-language "here is the one to pick."
